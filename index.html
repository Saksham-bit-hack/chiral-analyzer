CIP Priority Rules — How R/S is determined
Step 1 — Rank Substituents
Assign priority 1–4 based on atomic number. Highest atomic number = highest priority (1). Ties broken by looking outward through bonds.
Step 2 — Orient Molecule
Point the lowest priority (4) group away from you. View the remaining three groups in order 1→2→3.
Step 3 — Assign Config
R (Rectus) = clockwise rotation 1→2→3. S (Sinister) = counterclockwise rotation 1→2→3.

Stereochemistry & Chirality
🔄
What is Chirality?
A molecule is chiral if it cannot be superimposed on its mirror image. The term comes from the Greek χείρ (kheir), meaning hand. Your hands are mirror images of each other — chiral molecules work the same way. The stereocenter is usually a carbon bonded to four different groups.
🏷
CIP R/S Rules
Cahn–Ingold–Prelog (CIP) assigns R (rectus, clockwise) or S (sinister, counterclockwise) to each stereocenter. Rank substituents 1→4 by atomic number. Orient the lowest-priority group away from you. Read the remaining three: clockwise = R, counterclockwise = S.
⚗️
Why Stereochemistry Matters
Thalidomide — the R-enantiomer is sedative; S causes teratogenicity. Ibuprofen — S-enantiomer is the active analgesic. L-DOPA treats Parkinson's; D-DOPA is toxic. Stereochemistry is critical to drug discovery and biochemistry.
↔️
E/Z Isomerism
Double bonds cannot rotate freely. E (entgegen, "opposite") — higher priority groups on opposite sides. Z (zusammen, "together") — same side. This replaces the older cis/trans nomenclature for most compounds and is determined by CIP priority at each doubly-bonded atom.
🧬
Enantiomers vs Diastereomers
Enantiomers — mirror images, all stereocenters inverted, identical physical properties except optical rotation. Diastereomers — stereoisomers that are not mirror images, differ in physical and chemical properties. A molecule with n stereocenters has up to 2ⁿ possible stereoisomers.
💊
Lipinski Rule of Five
Christopher Lipinski's heuristic for oral bioavailability: MW ≤ 500 Da, LogP ≤ 5, H-bond donors ≤ 5, H-bond acceptors ≤ 10. Compounds passing ≥ 3 rules tend to be orally bioavailable. Exceptions exist for natural products and biologics.
📐
TPSA & Permeability
Topological Polar Surface Area (TPSA) — sum of surfaces of polar atoms. TPSA < 60 Å² predicts good CNS penetration. TPSA < 140 Å² predicts good oral absorption. TPSA > 140 Å² suggests poor membrane permeability.
🧮
LogP & Lipophilicity
LogP = log of the partition coefficient between octanol and water. Measures lipophilicity. LogP 0–3 often ideal for drugs. Too high (>5) → poor solubility, toxicity. Too low (<−2) → poor membrane penetration. Related: LogD accounts for ionization at physiological pH.
🔬
SMILES Notation
SMILES (Simplified Molecular-Input Line-Entry System) encodes molecular structure as a string. Atoms by symbol, bonds by notation. @ and @@ encode tetrahedral chirality. / and \ encode double bond geometry. Isomeric SMILES includes stereochemistry; canonical SMILES is standardized.
SMILES Notation Reference
CCarbon atom (aliphatic). Uppercase = non-aromatic. Hydrogen count inferred by valence.
cAromatic carbon. Lowercase = aromatic atom. Must be part of aromatic ring.
=Double bond. C=C is an alkene, C=O is carbonyl. No symbol = single bond.
#Triple bond. C#C is alkyne, C#N is nitrile.
@Tetrahedral chirality — anticlockwise (S in most cases). [C@@H] is clockwise from hydrogen side.
/\Double bond geometry. C/C=C/C is E (trans). C/C=C\C is Z (cis).
[]Bracket atoms — for charged, isotope-labeled, or unusual valence atoms. [NH4+], [13C], [Fe+2].
1–9Ring closure digits. C1CCCCC1 = cyclohexane. Matching numbers form a bond.
